## Step one: the visualization

I decided to choose this data because off the bat I was having trouble understanding what I was supposed to derive from it. Although the Data has some perk, which is the fact that its easy to read and has great color use- I still had some questions about interpretation. I also was able to pick out who the intended audience was and also after some thought, understood its purpose. From reading the title, I immediately thought that it did not do a good job as explaining what the chart was actually showing and it also is not cohesive with the article. As I thought more deeply, I realized that the chart is missing information. If we are going to compare how person's spending changes in December then we need add information to actually compare it too. This chart is a bit misleading because what exactly is the reference point? Are we looking at the full picture? I also think that it is important to note that the chart does have a comparison, but it is not the comparison that the title implies. Additionally, I thought the chart design with the colors scheme and distinction were on point. However, since the data wants to compare a decrease having a true zero line would better depict that. I also felt tat the subtitle overlaps with the x axis. Having that reduced would help alot in the simplicity of the data.

## Step two: the critique
In starting this redesign process, my biggest red flag with this data set is that it was missing information. I thought that if I could fill the gap with what they are missing, this will make the data more interpretable. The color scheme and layout work well. Using red and green made it easy to interpret what is good and bad. Having the highest value on top also makes the chart intuitive and easy to scan. As mention above The title is less effective. The title talks about making a specific comparison between a single category over time while the chart talks about the comparion between catergories.  So much so that I wanted to create a redesign that mirrored that. Using Stephens Fews Data Viz profile, was really helpful in deducting what could be better. What I felt stuck out to most was the truthfulness of the chart. I will explain more in my sketched out soltuiom pootion but, The article and title of chart not really being aligned made it clear that this chart could be misleading.

I also wanted to mention that the everyday audience played a key role in my redesign process. I kept thinking about if I was a average shopper what would this graph tell me. So I knew that even if I was going to change the chart design, I would still have to find one that was simply enough for a general audience. This charts strength is its simplicity, and I did not want to do anything more that would to take that away. Beside the redundancy in the wordigns, I also thought adding percentages on top of the bar would be helpful. The everyday audience reading the chart should not have to squint or use the fingers as pointers to figure out where something lies. The more I looked at the chart the more it became harder to figure out what to fix.

## Step three/four: Sketch a solution and test it
In this step, I was fully fixated on trying to show the need for a comparison. So I came up with a chart that changed the title too "How much more or less do we spend in December." This title would speak directly to what I my reimagined design would look like. I also added a legend that highlight the color scheme and was labeled "December increases", "December decrease" and in grey "May or typical month." In this redesign I was focused on showcasing the other months in comapoeison to December. I felt as though this made a lot of sense because, now a reader can look at the chart and see how much more or less people are spending in December versus a regular month. I used a group horizontal bar chart to showcase this comparison. I also added -20 onto my chart, after 0. I also put my catergories in the x axis and my percentages (although I wanted to change them to pounds like what is being talked about) in my y axis.

When it was my turn to show my design I felt like my group mates took a while to really interpret what I had drawn. That was not a good sign. Once everyone was able to review it thoroughly, one person stated that they liked my colors scheme. They were able to quickly figure out what was good and what was bad. They also thought parts of my legend were pretty clear. Some critiques that I was given was that I should instead use a true zero line to depict the negative data. It wasn't as interpretable as first, and this would help readers visually see what I am trying to say. (Although the original chart did use a true zero line, I wanted to flip the chart horizontally for more readability and did not realize I had did the true zero line incorrectly). They also added that my title could be a bit more specific. They suggested that I add in comparison to a typical month. So Now it would be "How much more or less do we spend in December in comparison to a typical month." I thought this enhanced the orignal chart as well because it is saying exacly what the chart is saying but with more spefcics. 

Another critiques that I was given was adding may or typical month was confusing. They wanted to know the significance of the month and if it needed to be there. They suggested that I instead leave it at typical month. I also want to say that in feedback it was mostly on how I could structure the chart better based on what I had told them of the original chart. For example, they agreed about the chart not having the full picture and my tip to compare it to another month (based on how I described the originalchart). The consensus from the group was that my chart was institutive and there was alot of good aspects. However, after speaking with the professor and relaizing I can not redesign this chart without the added data that I say it needs, I realized that I may be overthinking this redesign.


I initially was so focused on what the title lacked in comparison to the data that I wanted to change up the data. While misinformation in data Viz does exist, I realized that the simple fix to this would be to match the title to the data. Instead for half of this assignment, I had the mindset to match the data to the title. Once I was able to finally come to that realization, I got rid of the group bar chart, kept the chart horizonal, changed up the title so that it made more sense to what the data was actually showing and added percentages on top of the bars. I still think that the title is misleading and with what the article talks about, it should be comparing spending in Decemeber across months not just categories.
