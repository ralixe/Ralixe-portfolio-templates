## Step one: the visualization

I decided to choose this data because off the bat I was having trouble understanding what I was supposed to derive from it. Although the Data has some perks, which is that its easy to read and has great color use- I still had some questions about interpretation. I also was able to pick out who the intended audience was and also after some thought, understood its purpose. From reading the title,I immediately thought that it did not do a good job as explaining what the chart was actually showing and it also is not cohesive with the article. As I thought more deeply, I realized that the chart is missing information. If we are going to compare how person's spending changes in December then we need add information to actually compare it too. This chart is a bit misleading because what exactly is the reference point? Are we looking at the full picture? I also think that it is important to note that the chart does have a comparison, but it is not the comparison that the title implies. Additionally, I thought the chart design with the colors scheme and distinction were on point. However, I also felt that the subtitle overlaps with the x axis. Having that reduced would help alot in the simplicity of the data.

## Step two: the critique
In starting this redesign process, my biggest red flag with this data set is that it was missing information. I thought that if I could fill the gap with what they are missing, this will make the data more interpretable. The color scheme and layout work well. Using red and green made it easy to interpret what is good and bad. Having the highest value on top also makes the chart intuitive and easy to scan. As mention above The title is less effective. The title talks about making a specific comparison between a single category over time while the chart talks about the comparion between catergories.  So much so that I wanted to create a redesign that mirrored that. Using Stephens Fews Data Viz profile, was really helpful in deducting what could be better. What I felt stuck out to most was the truthfulness of the chart. The article and title of chart not really being aligned made it clear that this chart could be misleading.

I also wanted to mention that the everyday audience played a key role in my redesign process. I kept thinking about if I was a average shopper what would this chart tell me. So I knew that even if I was going to change the chart design, I would still have to find one that was simple enough for a general audience. This charts strength is its simplicity, and I did not want to do anything more that would to take that away. Beside the redundancy in the words, I also thought adding percentages on top of the bar would be helpful. The everyday audience reading the chart should not have to squint or use the fingers as pointers to figure out where something lies. Truthfully, the more I looked at the chart the more it became harder to figure out what to fix.


## Step three/four: Sketch a solution and test it
In this step, I was fully fixated on trying to show the need for a comparison. So I came up with a chart that changed the title too "How much more or less do we spend in December." This title would speak directly to what I my reimagined design would look like. I also added a legend that highlight the color scheme and was labeled "December increases", "December decrease" and in grey "May or typical month." In this redesign I was focused on showcasing the other months in comparison to December. I felt as though this made a lot of sense because, now a reader can look at the chart and see how much more or less people are spending in December versus a regular month. I used a group vertical  bar chart to showcase this comparison. I kept the true 0 on my chart and put categories in the x axis and my percentages (although I wanted to change them to pounds like what is being talked about in the article) in my y axis.

When it was my turn to show my design I felt like my group mates took a while to really interpret what I had drawn. That was not a good sign. Once everyone was able to review it thoroughly, one person stated that they liked the colors scheme. They were able to quickly figure out what was good and what was bad. Which confirmed that I didn't need to change this design feature. They also thought parts of my legend were pretty clear. Some critiques that I was given was that I should instead use a true zero line to depict the negative data. It wasn't as interpretable as first, and this would help readers visually see what I am trying to say. (Although the original chart did use a true zero line, I wanted to flip the chart vertically for more readability and did not realize I had did the true zero line incorrectly). They also added that my title could be a bit more specific. They suggested that I add in comparison to a typical month. So Now it would be "How much more or less do we spend in December in comparison to a typical month." 

Another critique that I was given was that adding "may or typical month" was confusing. They wanted to know the significance of the month and if it needed to be there. They suggested that I instead leave it at typical month. I also want to say that in the feedback it was mostly on how I could structure the chart better based on what I had told them of the original chart. For example, they agreed about the chart not having the full picture and my tip to compare it to another month (based on how I described the original chart). The consensus from the group was that my chart was institutive and there was a lot of good aspects. However, after speaking with the professor and realizing I can not redesign this chart without the added data that I say it needs, I realized that I may be overthinking this redesign... Instead of adding a comparison to match what the title implied, I should adjust the title to reflect the comparison the data already makes.


I initially was so focused on what the title lacked in comparison to the data that I wanted to change up the data. While misinformation in data Viz does exist, I realized that the simple fix to this would be to match the title to the data. Instead for half of this assignment, I had the mindset to match the data to the title. Once I was able to finally come to that realization, I got rid of the group bar chart, kept the chart vertical, changed up the title so that it made more sense to what the data was actually showing and added percentages on top of the bars. I still think that the title is misleading and with what the article talks about. It really should be comparing spending in December across months not just categories. 



## Step five: build the solution

  As you can see here, I have made the chart vertical; adding the categories at the bottom and the percentages on top. I changed the chart title to "What do we spend more or less on in December." This title is much more clearer and captures the percentage changes that the chart already gives. It has makes it clear with the different length of the bars, which category they spend more and less on. This redesign is clearer and intuitive. I like this redesign because it is the definition of "less is more." I want to mention that, If the team who wrote the article and design the data viz asked for my input, over all I would add the comparison. It would help the information that they are trying to communicate in the article land better. They also talked about the spending in pounds. Adding that instead of percentages, could have also went a long way. I would still also suggest changing the title, adding the data and removing the redundancy. Above all, this data viz is what its suppsoed to be; Simple. (always room for growth though :)
